Recipe scaler: halve, double, or resize any ingredient list

Paste the ingredients, say how many servings the recipe makes and how many you want, and every amount is rescaled into measures you can actually scoop: half of 1/3 cup comes out as 2 tbsp + 2 tsp, not "1/6 cup". Switch to metric or US units if you like. Nothing you paste leaves your browser.

    How the amounts are rounded

    • Cups and spoons: if the new amount is a standard measure (1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 3/4 cup; whole or half tablespoons; 1/8 to 7/8 tsp) you get that. Otherwise it's spelled out with the fewest scoops from a normal set of cups and spoons: 3/4 cup x 1 1/2 = 1 cup + 2 tbsp. Amounts that don't come out even are rounded to the nearest 1/8 teaspoon and marked with ~.
    • Other amounts (eggs, cloves, cans, ounces, grams) are scaled and shown in kitchen fractions, or whole numbers from 10 up. In "1 (15 oz) can", only the count scales; the can size stays.
    • Metric: mL and grams are rounded to the nearest 0.5 under 10, to whole numbers up to 100, then to the nearest 5; liters and kilograms to 0.1. The spoon math matches the printable kitchen conversion chart, which has the common halving and doubling amounts on one page.
    • Lines without a number at the start ("salt to taste", "For the sauce:") are kept as they are.

    What scaling can't do for you

    • Eggs: if you get half an egg, beat one egg and use about half of it, or round to a whole egg.
    • Pans and times: a bigger batch may need a bigger pan or two pans, and cooking times don't scale with the amount. Go by the recipe's doneness cues (golden, set, a tester comes out clean).
    • Cups are volume, not weight. "US cups and ounces" converts mL to cups and grams to ounces; it doesn't turn a cup of flour into grams, because that depends on the ingredient and how the cup is filled.

    Where the numbers come from

    1 cup = 16 tbsp = 48 tsp. Metric factors from NIST Special Publication 811, Appendix B.9 (1 cup = 236.5882 mL, 1 tbsp = 14.78676 mL, 1 tsp = 4.928922 mL, 1 oz = 28.34952 g, 1 lb = 453.5924 g): NIST SP 811, Appendix B.9.
